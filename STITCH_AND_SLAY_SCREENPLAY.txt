STITCH & SLAY
A Screenplay by Kaedra + Rhea Noir

INT. JADA'S WORKSHOP - NIGHT

**INT. JADA'S WORKSHOP - NIGHT**

**MACRO CLOSE-UP:**

A thick, industrial needle PIERCES through iridescent EVA foam with a wet, rhythmic *THWACK*. The thread—heavy-duty nylon—creaks under the tension. 

JADA (24, sharp eyes, skin the color of dark roast coffee) has charcoal dust under her fingernails and silver paint smeared across the cheekbone of her tired but focused face. 

She wears an oversized, salt-stained Iverson jersey and a lavender silk hair wrap. She’s barefoot. 

**WIDER:**

The workshop is a basement sanctuary. Amber light from three different desk lamps pools over a central industrial workbench, fighting against the rhythmic, ghostly blue flicker of a 3D printer in the corner. 

Racks of half-finished capes—velvets, synthetics, leathers—hang like ghosts. A wall is plastered with reference photos: Janelle Monáe in futuristic chrome, ancient West African bronze masks, and schematics for Valkyrie flight-suits.

Jada grunts, wrestling with a massive breastplate. It’s the "Cyber-Valkyrie" rig. She picks up a HEAT GUN. The coil glows orange. 

The *WHIRR* of the fan fills the room. She blasts the foam, the heat making the material malleable. With calloused hands, she forces the curve, holding it until her muscles tremble.

JADA
(Muttered, breathless)
Stay, you stubborn ass jawn. Stay...

The foam cools into a perfect, aggressive arc. She exhales, the breath hitching in her chest.

JADA (V.O.)
Forty hours of sanding for a thirty-second walk-on. If this seam doesn’t hold the tension of the LEDs, the whole rig’s gonna blow a fuse at the Con. Then what? All this sweat for a "nice try" tweet? 

She stands up, joints POPPING like bubble wrap. She moves to a cracked, floor-length mirror propped up by milk crates. 

Next to the mirror is "THE DOUBLE"—a mannequin molded exactly to Jada’s measurements. No standard retail proportions here. Wide hips, sturdy thighs, a real-world waist.

Jada lifts the breastplate and presses it against her own torso. She adjusts the nylon straps, cinching them over the Iverson jersey. 

She looks at herself. Really looks. She shifts her weight, checking the silhouette. The armor doesn't hide her curves; it cages them in silver-etched power.

JADA
(To her reflection)
Aight. Okay. We seeing it now. No cap, this silhouette is lethal.

She strikes a pose—back straight, chin up. For a second, the exhaustion vanishes. She isn't a tired girl in a basement; she’s the vanguard of a dead empire.

*PING.*

The sound is sharp, digital, and annoying. It comes from a battered MacBook perched on a stack of storage bins.

Jada drops the pose. Her shoulders slump. She trudges over and swipes the trackpad with a knuckle to keep the paint off the keys.

**ON SCREEN:** An email from a commission client. 
*“Hey Jada, following up on the 'Star-Knight' gauntlets. Found a guy using Midjourney/3D-Print workflows who can do a 'similar vibe' for $50. Can you match or should I go with the AI-gen route?”*

Jada’s jaw tightens. Her teeth grind. 

JADA
Fifty dollars? For a whole-ass gauntlet?

She stares at the screen. The blue light makes her look hollow. 

JADA
Deadass? Go ahead then. Go get your plastic trash. See if that "vibe" holds up when the light hits it. See if it got a soul.

She doesn't reply. She slams the laptop shut. The silence of the basement feels heavier now. 

She turns back to the Valkyrie suit. She reaches into the interior of the chest piece, her fingers dancing over a hidden terminal of copper wiring and soldered chips. 

JADA
(A whisper, defiant)
Let 'em try to prompt *this* into a generator. You can't code soul. 

She flips a micro-toggle.

*CLICK.*

A low HUM vibrates through the room. Starting from the "heart" of the breastplate, a deep, rhythmic violet light begins to PULSE. It travels through the etched grooves of the foam like blood through veins. 

The violet glow hits Jada’s face. It’s hypnotic. The "Masterpiece" is breathing.

Jada stares at it for a long beat. The anger from the email fades, replaced by a cold, hard pride. 

She collapses into her creaky swivel chair. She cracks her knuckles—*pop, pop, pop*—and just looks at the suit. 

She’s spent. Her back aches. Her eyes burn. But she’s winning.

JADA
(To the suit)
We gonna movie-star ‘em, girl. They ain't even ready for the slay.

She reaches for her phone. The screen-light illuminates her fierce, tired smile. She opens the camera app, holds the phone up, and frames a shot: her silhouette, dark and jagged, against the pulsing violet glow of the armor.

She types a caption: *STITCHED. NOT PROMPTED. SEE Y'ALL AT THE TOP.*

**FADE OUT.**

**CLOSING IMAGE:**
Jada, small but immovable, silhouetted against the glowing Valkyrie armor. The warrior she built towers over her, but Jada is the one holding the power.

INT. JADA'S WORKSHOP - NIGHT (CONT'D)

[SCENE START]

**INT. JADA'S WORKSHOP - NIGHT (CONT’D)**

The workshop is a cavern of shadow, lit only by the electric neon of JADA’S (24, Black, a force of nature in a tank top) dual-monitor setup. 

The blue light catches the sweat on her brow, turning her forehead into a topographical map of a digital world. The silence is heavy, smelling of spray paint, burnt flux, and old coffee.

*DING.*

The "New Message" chime cuts through the air like a gunshot. 

Jada doesn’t move for a beat. Her hands—stained silver at the knuckles, palms mapped with tiny needle-prick scars—hover over a soldering iron. She sets it down on a heat-proof mat and turns to the screens.

She clicks. The TECHCORP logo—a "TC" so silver and sleek it looks like it was bled from a robot—fills the display.

**JADA (V.O.)**
(low, a prayer)
Please let this be the one. Please don’t be playing with me.

She scrolls. The subject line: *PARTNERSHIP PROPOSAL: THE FUTURE OF “JADACRAFT.”*

Her eyes widen. She hits a section titled *COMPENSATION*. Her breath hitches. 

**JADA**
Deadass? 

She scrolls faster. The numbers are staggering. It’s more than her Patreon, her Etsy, and her weekend shifts at the fabric store combined for a year. It’s "move out of the hood" money. It’s "retirement for Nana" money.

Then, she hits the fine print. *“Digital Asset Synthesis.” “AI-Integrated Design.” “Full-Body Volumetric Ownership.”*

Jada’s jaw tightens. Her hand clenches into a fist, the silver paint cracking on her knuckles. She reaches for her phone and hits a speed-dial.

On the third ring, MARCUS (25, Black, sharp, the kind of guy who can fix a server and a car with the same wrench) picks up. In the background, the rhythmic, high-pitched *whir-click-whir* of a 3D printer hums.

**MARCUS (V.O.)**
(through speakerphone)
Yo, J. You see the time? I’m mid-print on that helmet—

**JADA**
Eighty-five thousand, Marc.

Silence. The 3D printer is the only thing speaking for five long seconds.

**MARCUS (V.O.)**
Say that again?

**JADA**
Eighty-five. To start. TechCorp. They sent the contract over two minutes ago. They want the "JadaCraft Aesthetic."

**MARCUS (V.O.)**
(voice dropping)
Jada... nobody gives away eighty-five racks for an "aesthetic." What’s the catch? Read the deliverables.

Jada leans in, her face inches from the screen.

**JADA**
They want a full scan. Every piece of the masterpiece armor. They want to digitize the patterns for their 1.0 Alpha rollout. Marc, they’re offering enough to get Titi out of that third-floor walk-up. I can’t sew my way into that kind of equity, no matter how fast I work.

**MARCUS (V.O.)**
Equity? J, they don't want your talent. They want your data. Once they got the scan, they don't need the seamstress. You’re building your own replacement. That’s digital sharecropping, sis. They take your seeds, grow the crop, and then charge you for the harvest.

Jada stands up, pacing the small radius of her chair. She kicks a stray spool of EVA foam.

**JADA**
Replacement? Please. This AI can’t even get a curve right without it looking like a glitch. I’ve seen their renders; they look like plastic junk. But the money? The money is real as hell. I’m tired of being "starving-artist" famous. I want "pay-the-bills" famous. 

**MARCUS (V.O.)**
And when they use your face and your style to sell a million skins to kids who don’t even know your name? When they "optimize" the Blackness out of your designs because the algorithm says it "scales better"? What then?

**JADA**
Then I’ll be watching them do it from a house with central air and a door that actually locks, Marc! No cap, I’m tired!

She stops pacing. Her eyes drift to her phone, resting on the desk. A notification from Instagram pops up. A comment on her latest progress reel.

*USER: GAmerGate99: Why you gotta make the armor so "urban"? The character isn't Black. Stop forcing your politics into cosplay.*

Jada stares at the screen. The comment is a tiny drop of poison in a sea of digital noise, but it stings because she’s exhausted. She looks back at the TechCorp offer. The massive dollar amount.

**JADA**
(voice cold)
They think I’m "too much" for the hobby? Fine. TechCorp wants to buy the vibe? Let 'em buy it. Maybe I need a shield. Maybe this bag is the only shield I’m gonna get.

**MARCUS (V.O.)**
(softly)
A shield stops things from getting in, J. It also keeps you from getting out. Don’t sign that jawn tonight. Just... sleep on it.

**JADA**
I’ll talk to you later, Marc.

She ends the call. The silence returns, heavier than before.

Jada walks over to the duct-tape dress form. The masterpiece armor—the breastplate—glows under the shop light. It is intricate, hand-tooled, featuring patterns inspired by West African geometry and Philly street art. It is a piece of her soul rendered in foam and paint.

She looks at her hand. 

She holds it up to the monitor, placing her palm near the "ACCEPT" button. 

The contrast is jarring. Her hand is textured, stained with the labor of a thousand hours, scarred and real. The screen is flat, perfect, and utterly cold. 

The cursor blinks. A heartbeat of light.

Jada’s finger twitches over the mouse.

**FADE OUT.**

[SCENE END]

INT. MARCUS'S APARTMENT - NIGHT

[SCENE START]

**INT. MARCUS'S APARTMENT - NIGHT**

CLOSE-UP: A rotary cutter SLICES through thick, brown butcher paper with a rhythmic *zip*. 

Steady hands guide the blade. These aren't just hands; they are a map of history—scarred knuckles, ink stains under the nails, a faint burn mark on the thumb from a 3D printer bed.

The camera pulls back to reveal MARCUS (28, spectacles, wearing a faded "Blerdcon 2019" tee). His apartment is a warm sanctuary of golden lamplight and organized chaos. A heavy oak dining table has been surrendered to spools of high-tensile thread and rolls of iridescent EVA foam.

He wears a wireless headset. His phone glows on the table.

**INT. JADA'S WORKSHOP - NIGHT (INTERCUT)**

Jada paces. The room is a stark contrast—cold neon blue, shadows stretching long across the floor. Behind her, the MASTERPIECE ARMOR stands on a headless mannequin. It looks like a god waiting to be birthed.

Jada holds her phone like a grenade she’s afraid to drop.

JADA
Yo, Marc. You busy? Or you still tryna be the Black Vera Wang of the Tri-state?

MARCUS
(A soft laugh, not looking up)
I’m just tryna get this bodice to sit right, Jada. What’s up? You sound... wiry.

JADA
I ain't wiry, I’m just... I’m lookin’ at this new carbon-fiber weave. If I hit the seams with that industrial cyanoacrylate, is it gonna fog the finish? I can’t have my jawn lookin’ dusty for the reveal.

Marcus stops cutting. He sets the rotary tool down. He knows that "voice." The way she talks technical when her heart is actually red-lining.

MARCUS
Jada. 

JADA
What? I’m deadass, I don't want the chemical reaction to—

MARCUS
Jada. Stop drawin’. You didn’t call me at midnight to talk about adhesive. What happened?

Jada stops pacing. She looks at her monitor. The TechCorp logo is a bright, corporate eye staring back at her.

JADA
I got the email, Marc. TechCorp. They didn't just invite me to the incubator... they offered me a full-scale partnership. 

Marcus goes still. He leans back in his chair, the wood creaking.

JADA (CONT'D)
It’s the bag, bul. For real, for real. They talkin’ six figures upfront. Licensing. A studio in the city. I could finally get out of this basement. I could pay off Ma’s medical debt and still have enough to buy a whole fleet of Bambu printers.

Marcus doesn't celebrate. He looks at his own work—the hand-stitched patterns, the soul in the physical craft.

MARCUS
Did you read the fine print? The "Digital Twin" clause?

JADA
(Defensive)
I read enough. They want to "preserve the process." It’s innovation, Marc. Don't start with that purist talk.

MARCUS
It ain't about being a purist, it’s about being a ghost. I’ve seen what they do. They don't want you, Jada. They want your measurements. They’re gonna scrape your stitch patterns, your silhouette logic, the way you blend tech with textile—and they’re gonna feed it into an AI generator. 

He stands up, pacing his small apartment, his voice getting tight.

MARCUS (CONT'D)
In eighteen months, there’s gonna be a "Jada-Style" button on their software. And you? You’ll be sitting in a studio they own, watching a machine do a version of your work that’s just hollow enough to be cheap. They ain't buying your craft, Jada. They’re buying the rights to bury it. You’re signing the death warrant for every Black girl coming up behind you with a sewing machine.

JADA
(Snapping)
That’s easy for you to say from the high ground! You got a stable day job at the firm, Marc. You got benefits! You got a 401k! I’m out here bettin’ it all on me, every single day. My rent don't care about "authenticity," and my landlord don't accept "soul" as a down payment. This is life-changing bread!

MARCUS
(Softly)
We’ve been here before. Remember the 2022 Expo? 

Jada flinches. The memory hits like a physical weight.

MARCUS (CONT'D)
We spent forty-eight hours straight in your kitchen. No sleep. Just Red Bull and the smell of hot glue. We built that entire Vanguard set by hand. And who did the judges give the "Innovator" award to? That kid from the suburbs who just prompt-engineered a 3D print and didn't even know how to finish a seam.

JADA
(Voice trembling)
And that’s exactly why I’m takin’ the deal! I’m tired of being the one workin’ ten times harder for a "Good Job" and a pat on the back while they hand the trophies to the people who took the shortcut. If I take this, I’m the one in the room. I’m the one with the power.

MARCUS
You won't be in the room, Jada. You’ll be the wallpaper.

Silence hangs between them, heavy and electric. It’s the kind of silence that only exists between two people who have almost said "I love you" a thousand times but said "Check this seam" instead.

JADA
(Quietly)
I gotta go. I need to think.

MARCUS
Don't let 'em erase you before you even get started. Please.

JADA
Aight, Marc. Goodnight.

MARCUS
'Night, Jada.

Marcus takes off his headset. He looks at his workstation. He reaches over and clicks off the desk lamp. 

**SPLIT SCREEN:**

**LEFT SIDE (MARCUS):** He sits in the dark. The only light is the faint streetlamp filtering through his blinds. He looks exhausted, his hands resting uselessly on his lap. He wants to help her, but he knows he can't compete with a six-figure check.

**RIGHT SIDE (JADA):** She stays in the neon blue. She turns toward her armor. The LED lights in the chest plate flicker—a tiny, unintentional glitch in the wiring. In the cold light, the masterpiece looks less like a triumph and more like a cage.

Jada turns back to her monitor. The cursor in the "Reply" box blinks.

*Blink.*
*Blink.*
*Blink.*

Like a heartbeat. Or a countdown.

[SCENE END]

INT. TITI'S HOUSE - DAY

[SCENE START]

**INT. TITI'S HOUSE - DAY**

The rhythmic *THUMP-THUMP-THUMP* of a 1950s cast-iron SINGER SEWING MACHINE is the only sound in the room. It’s a mechanical heartbeat. 

Sunlight cuts through yellowed lace curtains, illuminating dust motes that dance over a bowl of steaming grits. The air is thick with the scent of Downy fabric softener and slow-simmering collard greens.

TITI (65) sits at the dining table. She’s regal in a floral housecoat and a silk headwrap, her weathered hands guiding a vibrant piece of KENTE-PRINT fabric under the needle with surgical precision.

Across from her, JADA (27) is a ghost of her online persona. She’s slumped in a mismatched wooden chair, swallowed by an oversized gray hoodie. Her "Masterpiece" armor is back at the apartment; here, she’s just a girl from North Philly with dark circles under her eyes.

Jada’s thumb flickers over her phone screen—a frantic, blue-light twitch.

JADA
(muttering)
TechCorp’s legal team sent over the revised PDF. Devon talkin’ ‘bout if I sign by Friday, the first wire hits Monday. Deadass, Titi. Six figures before I even stitch a hem.

Titi doesn’t look up. *THUMP-THUMP-THUMP.* 

JADA (CONT'D)
I’m talkin’ real ‘get out of the trenches’ money. I could get you that spot in Cherry Hill. No more leaky pipes. No more—

Titi clicks her tongue—a sharp, percussive sound that cuts through the machine’s rhythm. She stops the pedal. Silence rushes into the room.

TITI
Fold that edge, Jada.

JADA
Titi, I’m tryin’ to tell you—

TITI
And I’m tellin’ you the bias tape is slipping. Put that glass slab down and help your elder turn this hem.

Jada sighs, a heavy, frustrated sound, and sets the phone face down. She leans forward, her fingers trembling slightly as she grips the fabric. 

TITI (CONT'D)
Now. What they want for all this "math" they giving you?

JADA
It ain’t math, it’s an AI sponsorship. They want to "optimize" my workflow. Scan my designs into their engine so the software can generate the base patterns. It’ll do all the tedious jawns—the scaling, the grading, the repetitive stitches. It’s efficient.

TITI
(snorts)
Microwave greens are efficient, too. Get ‘em hot in three minutes. But they don't feed your spirit, and they sure as hell don't taste like home. 

JADA
It’s not the same. This is tech. It’s progress.

TITI
If a machine makes it, Jada, it don't got no ghost in it. You sellin' the clothes, or you sellin' your ghost?

Jada flinches. She looks at the phone, then back at Titi’s steady gaze.

JADA
They want to scan *me*, too. Not just the work. A full-body digital twin for the "global avatar market." 

Titi narrows her eyes, sensing the hesitation in Jada’s voice.

JADA (CONT'D)
Elena—the rep—she said the algorithm works better with "standardized proportions." They want to... slim the digital version down. Just a little. To make it "aspirational." 

Titi lets out a dry, humorless laugh. She reaches across the table and catches Jada’s hands in hers. Titi’s palms are calloused, warm, and unshakable.

TITI
Aspirational? Girl, you are built like a cathedral. You got your grandmother’s hips and my shoulders. That thickness is the architecture of your ancestors. It’s survived Jim Crow, the walk from Down North, and every hardheaded man in this city. It ain’t a "bug" to be fixed by some boy in a cubicle.

JADA
(softly)
They say it’s what the market wants.

TITI
The market wants to eat you without having to look at you. Your great-grandmother, she used to stitch messages into quilts for the folks heading North. Map lines hidden in the embroidery. To the white folks, it was just a blanket. To us, it was a map to the promised land. For us, making things by hand wasn't no hobby. It was survival. It was saying "I am here" when the world tried to say we weren't.

Jada looks down at her hands, the same hands that spend eighteen hours a day wiring LEDs into EVA foam and hand-sewing silk linings.

JADA (V.O.)
I spend all my time trying to build "Masterpieces" to prove I'm the best. But Titi makes me feel like I'm already the prize.

Titi reaches into her sewing basket and pulls out an old, heavy steel SEAM RIPPER. The metal is worn smooth from decades of use. She presses it into Jada’s palm.

TITI
If you take that money—and I know you might have to—you don't go in there as a "creator." You go in there as a maker. 

JADA
What’s the difference?

TITI
A creator thinks they made the world. A maker knows how to take it apart. 

Jada closes her fingers around the cold steel. It feels heavier than the phone. More solid.

TITI (CONT'D)
You take their money. You build your little "twins." But you keep that ripper close. If they try to stitch you into a shape that don't fit, you be ready to rip the seams out their whole system from the inside. Understand?

Jada looks at the seam ripper. She feels the weight of it. A slow, dangerous smile spreads across her face. The "vibrate" mode on her phone goes off on the table, but she doesn't even glance at it.

JADA
(low, firm)
Understood. No cap.

Jada stands up. She looks taller. The hoodie doesn't hide the power in her frame anymore; it just contains it. She slides the seam ripper into her front pocket like a concealed blade.

JADA (CONT'D)
I gotta go. Marcus is waiting on the final renders for the TechCorp pitch.

TITI
Go on then. And Jada? 

Jada pauses at the door.

TITI (CONT'D)
Don't you dare come back here talking 'bout no "standardized proportions." You're a masterpiece, girl. Act like it.

Jada nods once—sharp, certain—and steps out into the Philly sun.

Behind her, Titi kicks the pedal. 

*THUMP-THUMP-THUMP.* 

The machine starts up again, louder now. It sounds like a war drum.

[SCENE END]

EXT. PHILADELPHIA CONVENTION CENTER - DAY

**EXT. PHILADELPHIA CONVENTION CENTER - DAY**

The morning sun bounces off the glass facade of the Convention Center, creating a blinding glare—the "Broad Street Burn." A SEPTA bus hisses past, belching exhaust into the humid air. 

*CLACK-CLACK. CLACK-CLACK.*

Two massive, heavy-duty rolling crates—midnight black with "JADACRAFT" stenciled in jagged gold graffiti—slam against the uneven pavement of the crosswalk.

JADA (24, a storm in a "STITCH OR DIE" hoodie) hauls the crates with white-knuckled intensity. She’s got oversized shades on and a garment bag slung over one shoulder like a rifle.

A wheel on the lead crate catches a jagged crack in the concrete. The crate tilts. Jada lunges, catching it before the masterpiece inside can shift.

JADA
(under her breath)
Deadass? Not today. Broad Street don’t give a damn about your EVA foam. Move, Jada. Move.

She heaves the crate back upright. Her bicep ripples under the hoodie. She’s breathing hard, sweat beads forming at her temples. 

The crowd around her is a chaotic blur. Half are traditional cosplayers—cardboard, duct tape, hand-painted leather—and the other half are "Plastic." They wear "TECHCORP PRO-SKINS"—flat, textureless spandex suits with AI-generated shading that looks perfect from ten feet away and soulless from two.

Jada eyes a "Pro-Skin" wearer nearby. The guy looks like a video game character that hasn't finished rendering. 

JADA (V.O.)
Look at this jawn. No texture. No heart. Just a 3D print and a prayer. 

Three young guys in high-end "Accuracy-Check" builds—gatekeepers of the scene—stand near the concrete pillars. They’re whispering, judging every person who walks by. Their eyes land on Jada’s crates.

AMARA (16, Black, wearing a DIY denim vest with anime patches) nudges her friend.

AMARA
(whispering)
Yo... look at the tag on that box. That’s JadaCraft. 

The whisper ripples through the nearby crowd like a current. *JadaCraft. The Philly Queen. The LED Goddess.*

Jada feels the shift in the air. The "Main Character" energy settles over her shoulders like a cape. She stands a little taller, ignoring the burn in her traps.

A tearing sound stops her. 

To her left, the PRO-SKIN GUY is panicking. The seam under his arm has split, revealing cheap white elastic underneath the "high-def" print. The AI-shading is warping, making him look like a glitched file.

PRO-SKIN GUY
(frantic)
No, no, no! This cost five hundred credits! It’s supposed to be "structural-mesh!"

Jada stops. She looks at her watch. She’s late. She looks at the guy. He’s pathetic. 

She reaches into a tactical pouch on her belt, pulls out a specialized curved needle and a spool of heavy-duty nylon thread.

JADA
Yo, back up. Stop pulling at it, you makin' it worse.

PRO-SKIN GUY
(startled)
I—I think the algorithm miscalculated the tension...

JADA
(deadpan)
The algorithm don't know your measurements, bruh. It knows a math equation. Hold your arm out.

Her hands move with terrifying precision. *Stitch-pull-loop.* She isn't just sewing; she’s reinforcing. 

JADA (CONT'D)
That TechCorp mess is just pixels on polyester. It ain't got no bones. You want a suit to hold? You gotta build the skeleton first. 

She snips the thread with her teeth and pats his shoulder. Hard.

JADA (CONT'D)
Aight, you’re good. For now. Try not to breathe too hard.

The guy stares at her, awestruck. Jada turns back to her crates, but Amara and her friends have closed in. Amara is vibrating with excitement, holding a phone out.

AMARA
Oh my god, JadaCraft? I’m such a fan! Your WIP on the Gram had me screaming! The way you did those LEDs? Revolutionary, no cap.

Jada’s exhaustion vanishes. She pivots. The "Brand" clicks into place. She drops her shades an inch, flashing a million-dollar Philly smile. 

JADA
Appreciate you, sis. Real recognize real. We don't do that "click-and-print" mess over here. We bleed for the seams. You building something?

AMARA
(beaming)
Trying to. I’m doing a custom Cyber-Punk Philly build.

JADA
Bet. Send me the progress pics. I wanna see that hustle. 

Jada poses for a quick selfie—strong, chin up, crates flanking her like gold-stenciled sentinels. Amara looks at the photo like she just met royalty.

*BUZZ. BUZZ.*

Jada’s phone vibrates in her hoodie pocket. She pulls it out.

**SCREEN: NOTIFICATION - DEVON COLE (TECHCORP)**
*"Jada, checking in. Our offer is only valid through the weekend. Don't let the 'old way' hold you back. Let’s talk about your future."*

Jada stares at the name. *Devon Cole.* The man who wants to turn her hands into a proprietary software.

JADA
(to herself)
Not today, Devon. Not today.

She shoves the phone back into her pocket, deep. 

She reaches the massive automatic sliding doors of the Convention Center. Through the glass, she can see the madness—the lights, the cameras, the thousands of fans waiting for a hero.

A blast of freezing AC hits her as the doors slide open, a violent contrast to the Broad Street heat. The roar of the crowd inside rises to a crescendo.

Jada grabs the handles of her crates. She doesn't look like a girl with a hobby. She looks like a gladiator entering the Coliseum.

JADA
(internal)
Aight, Philly. Let's show 'em how we do.

She steps over the threshold. The wheels of her crates scream on the polished linoleum, cutting through the noise. 

**FADE OUT.**

INT. CONVENTION FLOOR - DAY

[SCENE START]

**INT. PHILADELPHIA CONVENTION CENTER - DAY**

MACRO SHOT: A pair of hands, knuckles scarred by needle pricks and fingertips stained with a silvery, conductive pigment. They move with the surgical precision of a clockmaker. 

JADA (24, Black, eyes like flint) snaps a glowing LED gauntlet into place. *CLICK.* The blue light bleeds through the gaps in a complex Kente-cloth weave layered over 3D-printed carbon fiber.
